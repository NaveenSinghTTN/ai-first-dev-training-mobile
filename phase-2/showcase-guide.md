# Phase 2 Showcase — StreamList App
### Your 15-Minute Demo Guide

---

Hi team,

As you wrap up Phase 2, we are running a 15-minute showcase for each developer. This is your chance to walk us through your StreamList build and — more importantly — share how you worked with AI to build it.

**This is not a code review. It is a demonstration of how you think and work with AI. The app is the evidence — your process is the story.**

Please read this guide fully before your session and structure your demo accordingly.

---

## The Format

15 minutes total. Stick to this order.

---

### 1. App Walkthrough — 4 minutes

Show the working app on a simulator. Keep it brief — this is just to confirm it runs. Cover all four screens in sequence.

Make sure you show:
- Home screen with genre filter working and content rows populated
- Navigate from a content card to the Detail screen and back
- Add a title to your Watchlist and show the badge update on the tab bar
- Watchlist screen — both the populated state and the empty state
- Run a search and show results appearing

If something is broken or incomplete — show it anyway. We will come back to it in section 5.

---

### 2. One Thing You're Proud Of — 3 minutes

Pick **one specific thing** in your build — a screen, a component, a technical decision — and walk us through it. Not the whole app. One thing.

Answer these three questions:
- What is it and why did you choose to highlight it?
- What was the prompt or sequence of prompts that produced it?
- What did AI get wrong on the first attempt, and how did you recover?

---

### 3. One Thing AI Got Badly Wrong — 3 minutes

Every developer will have at least one. Pick the most instructive failure in your build.

Answer these three questions:
- What did you ask for, and what did AI produce instead?
- How did you diagnose what went wrong — was it the prompt, the context, the rules file?
- What did you change, and did it fix it?

> This section carries real weight in the showcase. A developer who can articulate failure and recovery shows more AI-first maturity than one who only shows what went right.

---

### 4. Your `.cursor/rules` — 2 minutes

Open your rules file. Pick 2–3 rules you are most glad you wrote.

Be ready to answer:
- Which rule saved you the most time or prevented the most mistakes?
- Is there a rule you wrote that AI kept breaking anyway?
- What would you add to the rules file if you were starting Phase 2 again?

---

### 5. What You'd Do Differently — 2 minutes

No prepared answer here — just honest reflection.

- One thing you would prompt differently knowing what you know now
- One architectural decision you would change
- One thing you wish was in the Phase 1 material or the StreamList spec

---

### 6. Questions — 1 minute buffer

---

## What We Are Looking For

| We want to see | We do not want to see |
|---|---|
| Specific prompt decisions and why | A tour of every file in the project |
| Evidence of iteration and recovery | "AI just generated this and it worked" |
| Honest reflection on failures | Only showing the polished parts |
| Rules that reflect real project thinking | A generic rules file copied from examples |
| Judgment calls defended with reasoning | Feature completion as the only metric |

---

## A Note on Polish

If your app has bugs or incomplete sections — bring them anyway. Explaining why something is not working and what your next prompt would be is more valuable than a polished demo with no reflection.

The prompt log you committed to your repo will be reviewed alongside the demo. If the log is thin, be prepared to walk through your process verbally in more detail.

---

## Before Your Session

Make sure the following are ready:

- [ ] App runs on iOS or Android simulator without crashing
- [ ] `.specstory/history/` (prompt logs) committed to your repo
- [ ] `docs/ADR.md` written with at least 3 decisions documented
- [ ] `.cursor/rules` committed and ready to show
- [ ] You have picked your one proud moment and one failure story in advance
